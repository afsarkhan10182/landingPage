# DigitalFuzed Marketing Operating Plan

This document is the source of truth for DigitalFuzed marketing, content, publishing, lead capture, and weekly review. Any agent working on marketing should follow this document before creating copy, videos, campaigns, landing pages, or replies.

## 1. Core objective

Help people recognise a problem in their daily work, show a practical way to handle it, and let them decide whether DigitalFuzed's approach is useful.

The business outcome is not views or followers. It is:

1. Problem recognition.
2. Useful education and trust.
3. Relevant conversations.
4. Optional demos, pilots, or customers.
5. Retention and referrals.

Do not force the audience into one product. Learn which product has demand and use that evidence to decide where to invest more.

### Current campaign focus

For the current campaign cycle, produce regular content only for these five products:

1. Salon Software (`saloon.digitalfuzed.com`)
2. School ERP (`school.digitalfuzed.com`)
3. Real Estate CRM (`realestate.digitalfuzed.com`)
4. CRM (`crm.digitalfuzed.com`)
5. ERP (`erp.digitalfuzed.com`)

The other products remain part of the DigitalFuzed portfolio and may appear in an occasional overview post, but they are not active campaign priorities until the focus list changes.

## 2. Positioning

### Umbrella message

Different industries carry different operational problems. DigitalFuzed helps teams handle those problems with practical working systems.

### Supporting message

When work is spread across paper, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, and memory, important details get missed. We show a clearer way to handle the work day to day.

### Problem-led approach

DigitalFuzed content must feel like practical help, not a software sales pitch:

1. Start with a recognisable industry problem or daily situation.
2. Explain what that problem causes: missed follow-ups, delays, duplicate work, unclear status, or lost information.
3. Show how the work can be handled more clearly using a real workflow.
4. Mention DigitalFuzed only as the practical system being demonstrated.
5. Offer a free live demo or on-call guidance as an optional next step, never as pressure.

Use language such as “Here is a clearer way to handle this” and “See how this workflow works.” Avoid “Buy now”, exaggerated promises, urgency, and feature dumping.

### Positioning boundary

Do not lead with “SaaS”, “platform”, “subscription management”, “tenant”, “admin panel”, or software-company architecture. Present each product as practical software for a specific industry or business workflow. Internal administration screens may help us operate the products, but they are not public marketing content.

### Proof standard

Show working products, real workflows, clear screenshots, and honest outcomes. Do not claim a feature, integration, price, customer result, AI capability, guarantee, or support policy unless DigitalFuzed has confirmed it. Show the customer-facing workflow, not internal SaaS administration.

## 4. Marketing model

Use one broad brand and many specific entry points.

| Layer | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DigitalFuzed brand | Establish breadth and credibility | “Business software for teams that are outgrowing manual work.” |
| Product content | Explain one product and one problem | “How a salon can reduce missed follow-ups.” |
| Product landing route | Convert interest | `digitalfuzed.com/salon-software` or a product selector |
| Lead capture | Record the person’s actual need | `product_interest=salon` |
| Follow-up | Continue the relevant conversation | Salon demo script, not a generic pitch |

The brand message can mention multiple industries. Each individual post should normally focus on one buyer, one recognisable problem, one practical workflow, and one low-pressure next step.

## 5. Channel model

Create one core content asset and adapt it for each channel.

| Channel | Role | Starting frequency | CTA |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Instagram + Facebook | Local discovery, practical education, and DMs | 5 short videos/week | Ask a question or see the live workflow |
| YouTube Shorts | Searchable problem and workflow library | 5/week | See the relevant workflow |
| LinkedIn | Founder credibility and business context | 3 posts/week | Discuss the business problem |
| X | Optional distribution and short lessons | 3/week | Read or discuss the practical lesson |

Do not create unrelated content for every platform. Reuse the core idea, then adjust the opening, caption, and CTA for the channel.

## 6. Content mix

Use the portfolio to keep the brand broad while keeping each asset understandable.

Start with this monthly mix:

- 30% product workflows: screen recordings and practical demonstrations.
- 20% industry problems: paper, missed follow-ups, billing, stock, and disconnected tools.
- 15% comparisons and education: what to check before buying software.
- 15% proof: approved customer stories, outcomes, or anonymised examples.
- 10% DigitalFuzed brand: team, approach, support, and behind-the-scenes work.
- 10% portfolio overview: introduce multiple products and help people choose.

Rotate product pillars. Do not give every product equal attention automatically; use qualified enquiries and demos to identify demand.

### Product rotation rule

Each week:

1. Cover two or three of the five active products.
2. Publish at least one portfolio-level post showing that DigitalFuzed serves multiple industries.
3. Repeat the strongest problem angle only when it creates clicks, conversations, or useful objections.
4. Give a product more content when it produces qualified interest, not merely high views.

## 7. Content formula

Every short video or post should answer five questions:

1. Who is this for?
2. What problem are they experiencing?
3. How can the work be handled more clearly?
4. What does the real workflow look like?
5. What optional next step would help the viewer?

For short video:

1. 0–2 seconds: specific, recognisable situation.
2. 2–7 seconds: what gets delayed, missed, or repeated.
3. 7–28 seconds: one real workflow or screen recording.
4. 28–35 seconds: explain how the workflow makes the work clearer.
5. Final seconds: a low-pressure invitation to view the demo or ask a question.

Use 9:16 video, 20–40 seconds, readable captions, and a real interface whenever possible. Avoid long logo intros and generic motivational content.

## 8. Product-interest capture

Every marketing route must record the product the person is interested in.

### Required lead fields

- Name
- Phone or email
- City and country
- Product interest
- Business type
- Current workflow or problem
- Source platform
- Campaign, post, or URL
- Date received
- Lead status
- Demo date
- Outcome
- Expected value or price discussion
- Paid or retained status

### Product-interest values

Use stable values in forms, links, and the lead tracker:

`school`, `hospital`, `realestate`, `restaurant`, `salon`, `crm`, `erp`, `qrb`, `custom`.

Do not use free-text product names when a controlled value is possible.

### Conversion route

Use one simple product selector or landing page that lets a person choose:

- I need a school system
- I need a hospital or clinic system
- I need a real estate system
- I need a restaurant system
- I need a salon system
- I need CRM or ERP
- I need QR pages or short links
- I need something custom

The selected product must be passed into the lead tracker and, where possible, into the WhatsApp or demo link using a UTM or equivalent source value.

Example:

`utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=portfolio&utm_content=salon-bookings&product_interest=salon`

## 9. CTA rules

Use one primary CTA per asset. Choose the CTA based on intent:

- Awareness: “See how this workflow is handled.”
- Interest: “Try the live demo.”
- High intent: “Tell us what part of the work is difficult.”
- WhatsApp: “Message us with the problem you want to handle.”

The live demo is free to explore. Support or on-call guidance can be discussed after someone shows genuine interest. Do not lead with price, pressure, or a promise that has not been confirmed.

Do not use vague CTAs such as “Learn more” when the relevant action can be named.

## 10. Lead response

Respond to genuine enquiries within four working hours.

First reply:

> Thanks for reaching out. Which product are you interested in, what city is your business in, and how are you handling this work today? I can show you the relevant workflow in a short demo.

Qualify on:

- Active business or credible project
- Product and problem fit
- Decision-maker involvement
- Team size, branches, or operating scale
- City and country
- Budget willingness
- Desired start date

A follower, like, or “price?” comment is interest, not yet a qualified lead.

## 11. 30-day operating cycle

### Week 1: Establish the portfolio

Introduce DigitalFuzed and publish one clear post for each major product group. The goal is to learn which industries respond.

### Week 2: Demonstrate workflows

Show one real workflow per selected product: booking, billing, attendance, patient flow, lead follow-up, inventory, reporting, or QR publishing.

### Week 3: Handle buying questions

Publish content about setup, staff adoption, data safety, support, pricing structure, onboarding time, and what the product does not do. Only use confirmed facts.

### Week 4: Convert and review

Repeat the strongest product/problem combinations, invite demos, review every lead, and choose the next month’s priority products based on evidence.

## 13. Measurement and decisions

Track metrics by product, platform, content piece, and source.

| Metric | Meaning | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watch time / 3-second hold | Hook quality | Improve the opening |
| Saves and shares | Problem relevance | Repeat the angle |
| Product-page clicks | Product interest | Improve the route or CTA |
| WhatsApp clicks | High intent | Check message and response speed |
| Qualified conversations | Buyer quality | Continue or narrow targeting |
| Demo attendance | Trust and follow-up quality | Improve reminders and proof |
| Paid pilots/customers | Commercial validation | Increase attention carefully |
| Retention/referrals | Product value | Improve onboarding and support |

Initial 30-day targets are working targets, not promises:

- 20 meaningful conversations
- 8 booked demos
- 3 paid pilots or customers

After 10 relevant posts, stop repeating an angle that creates views but no clicks, conversations, or useful objections. Keep a product in rotation when it creates intent, even if its reach is smaller.

## 14. Advertising rule

Do not run broad paid campaigns until organic content identifies product-market interest. Once one product and audience show qualified demand, test a small geographic campaign with one product, one buyer group, one problem, one landing route, and one tracked CTA.

Prefer high-intent search campaigns before broad cold social campaigns. DigitalFuzed must own the ad accounts, tracking, creative source files, and lead data.

## 15. Required approvals and facts

Before publishing or launching a campaign, confirm:

- Product name, URL, and supported workflow
- Target buyer and geography
- Final price, pilot terms, and onboarding time
- Demo or WhatsApp route
- Product access with safe sample data
- Approved screenshots, recordings, logo, and brand assets
- Support policy and response owner
- Confirmed integrations and limitations
- Customer proof and written permission to publish it
- Claims that must not be used

Never publish real customer data, passwords, private conversations, or unapproved testimonials. Never give social-account passwords in chat; use delegated access or an approved secure connection.

## 16. Source-of-truth rules for agents

Any agent working on DigitalFuzed marketing must:

1. Read this file before proposing or producing marketing work.
2. Preserve the umbrella-brand and product-interest-capture strategy.
3. Check the product portfolio table before using a name, URL, or feature.
4. Use one clear product focus per content asset.
5. Include a measurable CTA and source tracking where possible.
6. Log assumptions and request confirmation for unverified facts.
7. Never publish, schedule, reply, or spend advertising budget without explicit approval.
8. Report results by product interest, not only by total reach.

## 17. Definition of success

DigitalFuzed marketing is working when the company becomes known for multiple practical software products and can answer, with evidence:

- Which industries are responding?
- Which product does each lead want?
- Which problems create the most qualified conversations?
- Which content and channel produce demos?
- Which products convert into paid pilots and retained customers?

If content creates attention but no product-specific intent, improve the offer, proof, CTA, landing route, or follow-up process before simply publishing more.
